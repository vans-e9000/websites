What Androsan handles

Work begins with the conditions that affect cost, access and delivery.

Before work starts, the brief needs a usable scope. That means understanding the location, ground conditions, access routes, drawings, materials, programme and the people who will use the finished work.

Androsan keeps these decisions connected to the site so changes are identified early and responsibilities remain clear.

What a site review considers

Ground and drainage

Known levels, soil information and water flow can affect site preparation, drainage design and the order of civil works.

Access and operations

Vehicle routes, deliveries, nearby activity and whether a site is occupied help define practical working limits.

Drawings and decisions

Current documents and a clear approval path help identify open questions before a work package is agreed.

Bring to the first conversation

A useful brief can start with five facts.

The first review becomes more accurate when the project information is clear.

  • LocationRegion, district, access route and site status.
  • ScopeWhat needs to be built, repaired, extended or coordinated.
  • DocumentsDrawings, schedules, surveys, approvals or available specifications.
  • TimingRequired start date, milestones and operational constraints.
  • Decision routeWho approves scope, cost, materials and changes.
